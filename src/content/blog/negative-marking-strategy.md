Many government exams take away marks for a wrong answer. The penalty varies by exam and can change between years, so always check the official notification of the exam you are preparing for. The mock tests on Abhyas use a penalty of 0.25 marks for each wrong answer and 1 mark for each correct answer. The ideas below work for any exam with a fractional penalty.

## The basic arithmetic

If a wrong answer costs a quarter of a mark and a right answer earns one, then a blind guess among four options has an expected result of:

- a one in four chance of gaining 1 mark, and
- a three in four chance of losing 0.25 marks.

That works out to roughly zero. A pure guess neither helps nor hurts on average. This is why the useful question is not "should I ever guess?" but "how much do I actually know about this question?"

## Eliminate before you guess

Once you can rule out even one option with confidence, the numbers move in your favour. With one option removed you have a one in three chance of being right. With two removed it is one in two. At that point the expected gain from answering is clearly positive.

So a good rule is:

1. **You know the answer:** mark it and move on.
2. **You can rule out two options:** answering is usually worth it.
3. **You can rule out one option:** answering is a small gamble, take it if you have time.
4. **You cannot rule out anything:** skip, and come back only if time is left.

## Do not guess in a panic

The worst guessing happens in the last two minutes, when you fill in everything that is left. That habit costs marks over a full exam. Keep a small time buffer and decide question by question using the rule above.

## Use the mark-for-review button

Our test screen lets you mark a question for review. Use it for questions where you have narrowed the choice to two options but are not sure. On the second pass you will have more time and a calmer head, and you can decide whether the risk is worth it.

## Practise this in mock tests

Negative marking is a skill, and you only build it under test conditions. In your next [mock test](/test-series/), keep a count of how many questions you answered by pure guess and how many by elimination. After the test, look at the result: did the elimination answers do better than the pure guesses? Most students find that they did, which is the evidence you need to trust the method.

You can also check how your accuracy changes over many attempts on the [My progress](/dashboard/) page. If your accuracy is low and you attempt almost everything, you are probably guessing too much. If your accuracy is high but you skip a lot, you may be able to take a few more calculated risks.
