Most aspirants solve previous year papers at the end of their preparation, once as a final test. That wastes the best thing a previous year paper can give you: a clear picture of what the exam actually asks. Used well, these papers shape your syllabus, your revision and your speed from the first month.

## Start earlier than you think

You do not need to finish the syllabus before you open a previous year paper. Attempt one paper in your first week, even if you score badly. The score does not matter yet. What matters is that you see the language of the questions, the mix of subjects and the level of difficulty. After that, every hour you study has a target.

You can find papers for every exam on the [previous year papers page](/previous-year-papers/) or by opening an exam, for example [SSC CGL](/exams/ssc-cgl/) or [IBPS PO](/exams/ibps-po/).

## Attempt it like the real exam

Set a timer and sit for the full paper in one go. Do not pause to look up an answer. On Abhyas the timer, question palette and negative marking work the way they do in a computer-based test, so you also get used to the screen.

A few rules that make the attempt useful:

- Attempt the paper in one sitting, with no phone and no notes.
- Mark questions you are unsure about instead of spending three minutes on them.
- Note the time when you finish each section.

## Review before you move on

The attempt is half the work. The review is where you improve. After the test, go through every question, not only the ones you got wrong:

1. **Wrong answers.** Was it a concept gap, a calculation slip or a misread question?
2. **Right answers you guessed.** Treat these as wrong. A lucky guess will not repeat in the exam.
3. **Right answers that took too long.** Find a faster method.

Write the reason for each mistake in one line. After three or four papers you will see the same two or three reasons again and again. Those are the things to fix first.

## Study by topic, not only by paper

Once you have solved a few papers, sort the questions by topic. You will notice that some topics appear in almost every year and others appear rarely. Spend more of your time on the topics that keep coming back. The subject list on each exam page, such as [SSC CGL](/exams/ssc-cgl/), gives you the broad sections to sort into, but always confirm the latest syllabus in the official notification.

## Do not memorise answers

Exams rarely repeat a question word for word. They do repeat ideas, patterns and types of questions. If you memorise that option C was correct, you learn nothing. If you understand why it was correct, you can solve the next question that uses the same idea.

## A simple weekly plan

- **Early weeks:** one paper a week, with a long review.
- **Middle weeks:** two papers a week, plus topic practice for your weakest areas.
- **Last weeks:** one paper every two or three days, then move to full-length [mock tests](/test-series/) in the exam's pattern.

Keep a record of your scores. The [My progress](/dashboard/) page saves your attempts on your device, so you can see whether your accuracy is moving in the right direction. For a short daily habit alongside papers, the [daily quiz](/daily-quiz/) takes five minutes.

Previous year papers will not guarantee a selection, and no practice material can. But attempted early, reviewed honestly and studied by topic, they are the most reliable guide to what the exam wants from you.
